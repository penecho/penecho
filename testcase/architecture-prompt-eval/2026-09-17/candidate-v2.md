## Architecture diagrams

Only for architecture diagrams/regions; other visuals, including sequence diagrams, keep their existing rules. Within this scope, override generic infographic density/panel suggestions with a topology-first view: **simple entities inside meaningful boundary frames, connected by explicit relationships; implementation details separate**.

* **Entities:** choose a consistent abstraction level of actors, components, services or stores. Use a compact name and at most one short role/type, optionally a meaningful icon. Features, actions, dimensions and configuration values are not entities. Keep paths, parameters, chips, bullet lists and explanatory prose out of topology nodes. Put necessary detail in a separate region keyed to the same names; do not repeat those facts in the map.
* **Frames:** enclose real subsystems, responsibility domains, environments or network/trust boundaries. Name each frame and leave padding for its title, children and connections. Distinguish nesting levels with restrained strokes/fills. Code ownership does not imply a process boundary; a local subprocess is not an external cloud service. Frames express containment, not merely full-width rows of module lists.
* **Relationships:** connect the actual participating entities, including important branches, independent entry paths and shared dependencies. Anchor each line to the correct shape and let its arrow tip meet the receiving outline. Explain direction consistently; distinguish calls, data, returns and dependencies. Label the action/data only where needed. Do not substitute whole-layer arrows or prose for required entity relationships, or invent links to make a neat chain.
* **Composition:** derive orientation, grouping, view count and detail placement from the system and question; no fixed template or path count. Make the topology the dominant readable visual. Use available space for entities and routing instead of placing a tiny diagram within large empty margins. Keep primary labels around normal 16–18 CSS px and annotations readable; avoid an arbitrary page-width ceiling or uniform page shrinking that makes them small. Move detail before shrinking type. Preserve essential relationships when simplifying.
* **Geometry:** derive node positions and ports from shared guides. Align comparable entities and parallel-edge labels; use regular gaps, consistent typography/strokes and deliberate exceptions for real conflicts. Reserve routing lanes sized for their labels. Wrap or shorten long labels, or widen their lane; never let them invade nodes or frame titles. Choose straight, orthogonal or curved routes to suit geometry. Crossings are not junctions. Use uniform SVG scaling: never stretch text or arrowheads with `preserveAspectRatio="none"`. Reflow/reroute when necessary; resizing must preserve attachment and legibility.
* **Review:** inspect the rendered topology without its detail region. Can the reader identify entities, understand containment, and trace the important paths endpoint to endpoint? Check actual display size, matching ports, arrow direction, peer/label alignment, text extents, collisions and clipping. Check counts and summary claims against the source. Correct semantic and geometric defects; do not judge by resemblance to a particular example.
